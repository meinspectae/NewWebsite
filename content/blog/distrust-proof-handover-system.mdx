In Dubai, deposits aren't won with goodwill. They're won with evidence.

At the Rental Dispute Centre, the strongest cases share the same pattern:

- Signed tenancy contract + Ejari
- Dual-signed move-in/move-out reports
- Timestamped, GPS-tagged photos
- A clear paper trail: emails, letters, invoices
- No "we'll sort it later" handovers

The weakest cases? Verbal agreements. Undated photos. Excel checklists without signatures. WhatsApp screenshots with no context.

Most deposit disputes don't happen because someone is acting in bad faith. They happen because the process is fuzzy. No standard checklist. No shared reference. No signatures. No timestamps. Then, 11 months later, both sides are arguing over memories and blurry images.

That's not a trust problem. That's a design problem.

## What a distrust-proof handover actually looks like

A distrust-proof handover has three layers:

1. A shared, room-by-room condition report
2. Time, place, and signatures — timestamp, GPS, and sign-off from both parties
3. A simple process everyone follows:
   - Complete the report before keys are issued
   - Export a PDF and email it to all parties the same day
   - Repeat at move-out using the original as the baseline
   - Attach invoices or receipts to specific line items for any deductions

In a recalibrating Dubai rental market, this isn't "nice to have." It's a competitive advantage.

## The uncomfortable truth

If your handover process collapses the moment trust breaks down, your process is the risk — not the other party.

- **Tenants:** without a signed, timestamped move-in report, you're handing the landlord a blank cheque for deductions.
- **Landlords:** without proof of pre-existing damage, you'll struggle to justify legitimate claims at the RDC.
- **Brokers and managers:** ad-hoc inspections invite disputes that a simple system could prevent.

The better question isn't "can I trust this tenant or landlord?" It's: do we have a signed, timestamped, photo-rich condition report? Is our workflow clear and repeatable? If this goes to the RDC, will our evidence hold up?
